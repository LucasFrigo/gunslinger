# Changelog

All notable changes to **Gunslinger VR** are documented here.

Format based on [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/).
Versioning follows [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/).

The current version is the single line in [`VERSION`](VERSION) (mirrored in `project.godot` → `application/config/version`). Code landing on `main` auto-bumps that string (agent rule + `.github/workflows/bump-version.yml`); `[Unreleased]` is only frozen into a dated section on a named release.

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- Duel title cards: before the standoff, each duelist gets a pixel-art name card while a fixed camera frames them in the arena, in flat and VR. Single player shows each NPC in turn, then you; a lobby shows every slot in order, NPCs included. You can walk, draw, and spin meanwhile; pulling the trigger skips the intro (for everyone in a lobby). A rematch of the same duel, Reset Duel, a gauntlet retry, Horde, and Practice skip it. Settings **Duel intro** turns it off (the host's setting counts in a lobby).
- Settings **Your Name**: the name on your title card and on LAN lobbies (empty uses the device name). Type it on desktop, or pick a western name in VR. A running Steam client's name still wins.
- Saloon bar: the bar on the west wall is now a final model. A black back bar has three arched mirror bays, rows of green and amber bottles, and a stepped crown. One bay is shot through. The counter has a cream top, gold capitals along its front, and a brass foot rail with spittoons. Two hanging-lamp lights pool on the counter, and the room's lamp and ambient light are paler and less orange, so the colours read truer. The room itself is final too: magenta walls over a black dado with a cream rail, and one plain magenta wall behind the duelist you face under the balcony. The street door is a real opening with louvred cream batwings hung shut in front of the solid outer doors, which are shut for the night with an iron hasp. Five windows are closed by louvred grey shutters, one slat missing, and the three on the east wall moved so none sits behind the east fighter's head. Four black beams with corner braces cross a white pressed-tin ceiling, coffered in the lit middle bay around a medallion. The plank floor has a cellar hatch behind the bar and two sprung boards. A shut black door leads to a back room. The two poker tables are final too: a green felt octagon in a black rim, chipped once, on a heavy black pedestal with four splayed feet, each with a game left on the felt (a deck, a dropped hand, stacks of checks, an amber bottle, two glasses), and a hanging-lamp light over each felt. The eight chairs are black spindle-back chairs with a curved top rail, turned uprights, a plank seat, and splayed legs on an H-stretcher. The chair on the door side of the near table lies on its back where it fell, and its collision lies down with it. The balcony is final too: a black gallery front with a cream bead and a row of turned cream balusters, sagging a little between two black posts that now frame the wall behind the far duelist, and a black stair with grey treads, one of them sprung. A gate across the foot of the stair keeps you off it, since the gallery is too close to the ceiling to stand on. The piano now stands against the east wall with its bench, the stove's pipe runs up to the ceiling, and the three low kegs at the end of the bar are full-height barrels with iron hoops (one tapped, one with its lid off), which now give crouch cover beside the near spawn. The fight left its mess between the near table and the end of the bar: a smashed chair, shards of mirror and bottle glass, black splinters, two broken bottles, and spilled chips and cards, some of it in the lane, all of it flat and none of it in the way of a shot. Only the smashed chair blocks you. The lamps are final too: a brass kerosene chandelier over the middle of the lane, a ring of six oil lamps whose cream glass shades and yellow flames glow in their own colour, one shade shot away and the whole thing hanging a little crooked, with a black smoke bell on a chain to the ceiling. Its open ring lets the light reach the middle of the ceiling, which was dark before, and its shadow falls on the ceiling. Black cone lamps hang on rods over the bar and on chains over the two tables. Other collision is unchanged.
- Glowing lamp glass and flames: arena surfaces named `M_LampGlass` or `M_Flame` draw exactly their own colour whatever the light, cast no shadow, and light nothing. Today that is the Saloon chandelier's shades, chimneys, and flames.
- Ink outline on guns, props, and ammo: the revolver, the coin, ace of spades, cigarette, and longneck, the practice bottle, and spent casings and loose cartridges wear the same dark ink line as the duelists, so what is in your hand matches the body. The bottle's line goes when it shatters. Brass only has a line for the newest 12 pieces within 6 m, and bullets have none. The line is thinner than the body's. F3 `object_outline_width` tunes it and `outline_enabled` hides it. Cosmetic only; the look is unchecked in a headset.
- Off-hand item persists: the last wheel pick (cigarette, coin, ace, bottle, or Empty Hand) is saved and put back in your hand, with no message, at the start of every duel, restart, gauntlet rung, Horde wave, and practice session, and again after you relaunch the game. The menu backdrop and the multiplayer waiting room stay empty-handed. A shattered bottle is replaced by a fresh one. There is no setting for it.
- Remote avatars show the off-hand item their player is carrying (held, thrown, or loose) in 1v1 multiplayer.
- Cartoon gun spin and twirl. In the headset, a spinning revolver turns about three times faster than before as a looks-only effect (catch, throw, and fire are unchanged). On flat, hold **Gun Twirl** (T, remappable in Settings; listed on the practice board) with the gun drawn to twirl it after the bell or in the practice hub; firing mid-twirl still shoots along your aim. Other players see your twirl in 1v1 and lobbies. A smear wedge under tapering ink arcs follows the barrel; F3 `spin_fx_mode` turns it Off or On, and F3 `spin_visual_mult` and `twirl_rate` tune the speed. AI duelists do not twirl. Net: new pose flag `GUN_TWIRL`.
- Hand-painted texture per GLB (tooling; no visible change until an image is painted). Each of the 24 scenario GLBs has fresh non-overlapping UVs on one 256×256 layout at one texel density and its own copies of its materials (`M_Wood__bld_bank`). Save `assets/models/scenarios/<map>/tex/<glb>.png`, run `dev/unwrap_per_glb_blender.py -- --link --glb <glb>`, and the GLB embeds the image and shows it in game with banded light, outline, and wind kept and grain off. A GLB with no image keeps its flat color. Ground and rock keep their tiling UVs and flat color.
- Photo cells and the sheet builder (tooling; no visible change until photos are added). `dev/build_trim_sheet.py` builds the scenery trim sheet from `assets/texture_src/strips/` and `cells/`, cut to one 32-color palette with padded borders, and writes a manifest of which cells have a photo. Doors, windows, signs, barrels, crates, troughs, tanks, and table tops take their cell when it has an image and keep their strip otherwise; `bld_<building>_<cell>.png` overrides a cell for one building.
- Trim sheet on scenery: walls, roofs, boardwalks, floors, fences, beams, and iron in every arena now take their color from a shared strip sheet (`assets/textures/trim_sheet.png`, a neutral placeholder for now) instead of one flat color per material, so wood, dark wood, whitewash, and car bodies share one siding color and the town looks more uniform. Ground and rock keep their flat color and grain. F3 **World** `sheet_mix` blends it (1 is the sheet, 0 the old look).
- Hurt vignette: every hit that lands on you pulls a red vignette in from the edge of the view, in flat and in the headset. An arm or leg hit is light, a torso hit stronger, a killing hit the strongest. F3 `hurt_limb`, `hurt_torso`, and `hurt_kill` tune it.
- Iris wipe: the kill cam starts and ends with an iris that closes to black ink and opens on the next view, in flat and VR, including the wave clear in Horde. F3 `iris_time` sets its length.
- Settings **Screen effects** toggle (on by default). Off turns off the iris, so the kill cam cuts hard, and the hurt vignette.
- Wind: outdoor arenas (Main Street, Canyon, Train Rooftop, Practice hub) have a looping wind bed, quietest on Main Street and loudest on the Train Rooftop, plus occasional gusts that sweep dust across the lane with a panned swish. The Saloon stays silent. The bed dips during the death cam and replay. Local only; F3 **Wind** has the volume, gust rate, and a gusts toggle. Placeholder audio until real recordings arrive.
- Bullet trail shader: the trail has a white-hot core, a soft edge, and a tail that thins out, in gold for every shooter, and is a little wider. It blends additive at dawn and dusk and mostly opaque at noon so it stays visible against the sky. F3 `trail_width` and `trail_tail_share` tune it.
- Sky: outdoor arenas get a small sun disc with a halo and flat cel clouds in place of the plain procedural sky. The gradient and fog stay as they were, so the horizon still fades cleanly. The clouds are different each load, take their tint from the sun, and drift slowly. The debug panel has a **Sky** group with `cloud_cover`, `cloud_speed`, and `sun_radius`.
- Skin edge light and hit flash: duelists get a rim of sun-colored light on their silhouette so they read against the fog at range, and the body blinks white for a moment on every hit, kills included, for NPCs, other players, and your own body. The kill-cam replay blinks at the same moments. F3 `edge_strength` and `edge_power` tune the rim.
- Ink outline: every duelist has a dark ink line around the silhouette so it reads at duel range through the fog, on NPCs, other players, your own body, and your hands. The line stays the same size on screen out to about 14 m, then thins, and it is cut by bullet holes along with the skin. Gibs are not outlined. F3 `outline_enabled` and `outline_width` tune it.
- World surface: arena ground, walls, and props share the banded light the duelists use, and sand, rock, and wood carry a fine grain that fades with distance. Scrub, yucca, hay, the saloon and porch signs, and the awnings sway in the wind and swing with each gust; the Saloon and the menu backdrop stay still. F3 **World** has `grain_strength_mult`, `grain_scale_mult`, and `sway_scale`.
- Banded light: duelists, their bullet holes, and their hands are lit in two flat bands that follow the low-poly facets instead of a smooth falloff. F3 `band_low` and `band_soft` tune it.
- Muzzle flash: every shot shows a billboarded orange star at the muzzle for a blink, different each shot, on the player, NPCs, and remote duelists.
- Barrel smoke: a thin grey wisp keeps curling off the barrel for a couple of seconds after every shot, on every shooter, and follows the gun. F3 `barrel_smoke_lifetime` scales its length (0 turns it off).
- Mixed lobbies: multiplayer lobbies of 2 to 4 duelists, any mix of players and NPCs, on LAN (every device, Quest included) and Steam (desktop only). Joining no longer starts a duel. Everyone stands in the arena as a waiting room (avatars and voice live, no hits) until the host presses **START**. Friendly fire is on. A lobby that is full or in a duel refuses the join and says why; the LAN list shows open slots (`1/2`) and greys out such a lobby, and the Steam list hides it.
- Lobby setup and Lobby page. Before you host, the Multiplayer page sets the lobby: how many duelists (2 to 4), free-for-all or Teams, the arena, and for each other slot a human or an NPC (a chosen enemy or Mixed, and in Teams its side). It is remembered for the next time you host. While hosting, the pause menu has a **Lobby** page, and as host it opens by itself when you start hosting and when the lobby fills. It lists each duelist with a colour chip, kind, side, and wins. The host can change the same settings there between duels, **Kick** a player, press START, or send everyone back to the lobby (**BACK TO LOBBY**); in Teams every player has a button for their own side and the page shows each side's wins. Restart Duel is hidden in the waiting room. After a duel the next one starts by itself 5 s later, or everyone returns to the waiting room if the lobby can no longer start (a client is told why). Messages say who joined, left, or was removed, who fouled, who was shot and by whom, and who won a free-for-all of three or four.
- NPC slots in a lobby. The host's game runs them and everyone else sees them move, draw, shoot, and fall like any other duelist. Each picks a rival of another side at the bell, other players included, and shoots back at whoever hit it. A slot set to Mixed rolls a new enemy every duel. A host can duel with NPCs alone. A player who leaves mid-duel counts as dead and is replaced by a Mixed NPC from the next duel; one who leaves in the waiting room just opens the slot. A host who has been shot can watch the NPCs fight on.
- Teams. A lobby set to Teams splits into two sides, any split with someone on each: 2v2 or 1v3 with four duelists, 1v2 with three. Players pick their own side and the host sets each NPC's; a pick made during a duel applies from the next one. The duel ends when one side has nobody left: a dead player's teammate fights on, and the result reads `YOUR SIDE WINS` / `YOUR SIDE LOSES`, `Last side standing`. NPCs never aim at a teammate or shoot back at one. The smaller side takes the first ring markers (a lone duelist stands on the player's spawn) and teammates take markers that follow each other on the ring. Every body wears its side's colour in Teams and its slot's colour in free-for-all, the same on every screen, instead of the grey mannequin and the NPC's own colour. Each win is counted for every duelist on the winning side, for the session.
- Duels of three or four players. With three or four duelists a first foul voids the standoff and names the fouler, and the same duelist's second foul puts them out. If you are shot before the duel ends, the camera orbits your body for a moment, then moves to your killer; fire / trigger steps through whoever is still alive, and it moves on by itself when the one you watch dies or leaves. A duelist put out by a foul goes straight to watching. The last kill plays the fly-along, the corpse orbit, and the replay for everyone, with the earlier bodies falling again at their own moments, and anyone's fire / trigger skips it for all.
- Voice chat in lobbies of three or four. Everyone hears each other from their own avatar. Once a duel is on, the dead hear only the dead and the living only the living, so a player who was shot cannot call out the shooter's position; a dead player's voice has no distance falloff for the other dead. Everyone hears everyone again in the waiting room, after the finish, and in the next duel. Voice in a two-player lobby is unchanged.
- Hit chunks: a hit on a duelist knocks a small patch out of the struck spot (head, torso front or back, upper arms, forearms, thighs, shins). A crater shows in the hole and the patch flies off as a gib that lands and stays until the next duel, with a flesh sound. The death-cam replay pops each chunk at the moment of the hit. New **Gore** setting (on by default) turns it off; F3 **Ragdoll / gore** has `chunk_chance`, `gib_speed`, and `gib_cap`. Mesh lab key 5 knocks chunks. Cosmetic only.
- UI text is translation-ready: menus, settings, pause, loading, HUD and reload lines, in-game messages, the prop wheel, the practice board, and the slot topper read from `translations/translations.csv` through `tr()` keys (English only; nothing looks different). `language` in `user://settings.cfg` forces a locale. New `--autotest=i18n` fails on a key missing from the CSV.
- Singleplayer menu: the Singleplayer button now opens a mode list (Gauntlet, Single Duel, Horde). Each mode has its own screen with a short description, its options, its best record, and a START button. Back steps one screen at a time. In VR the mode and START buttons are bigger.
- Gauntlet best record: the furthest duel cleared, the best score, and whether you have beaten the ladder are saved after a win or a loss and shown on the Gauntlet screen.
- Horde mode: endless single-player survival on a SP page arena you pick once per run. Every wave opens with a standoff and a bell, up to 3 NPCs, all focused on you with no retaliation between them — crossfire still lands physically. Clearing a wave plays the kill-cam fly-along and slow-mo with no replay, then heals you, refills your cylinder, and snaps the gun to the holster without moving you, before the next standoff. Dying ends the run like a free-duel loss (kill cam, orbit, replay). 8 authored waves, then the table loops with every slot promoted an archetype tier and every NPC a little faster. Kills and headshots score, wave clears add a bonus, crossfire kills count toward the clear but score nothing. Best waves cleared and best score are kept separately and shown on the SP page. Pause has **RESTART HORDE**.
- Free duel against one to three NPCs. The singleplayer free-duel row has an **Opponents** pick and a **Mixed** enemy that rolls each slot, and it remembers your last arena, enemy, and count. They stand on per-arena markers and fight free-for-all: each picks a random target at the bell and shoots back at whoever hit it. Shots pass through corpses. A mid-fight kill is a ragdoll with an `N left` message; the last one standing wins and the replay shows who really fired, with the earlier corpses falling again. If you die, the survivors shoot each other during the corpse orbit.
- Gauntlet rung 7, "Mexican standoff": Ghost, Sheriff, and Drunk together on Main Street.
- Ragdoll: a killing shot drops the NPC, your body, and another player's avatar in multiplayer as a rag doll, kicked along the shot at the limb that was hit. The gun falls loose. A coin, ace, or bottle in the off hand falls too; a cigarette is hidden. The replay plays the whole fall after the death, slowed with the rest of the tail. A foul does not collapse. F3 has a **Ragdoll** group (`ragdoll_enabled` 0 turns it off), and mesh lab key 4 drops the puppet for a look.
- F3 **Show hitboxes** toggle draws every combatant's hit volumes (head red, torso orange, arms blue, legs green).
- VR spin throw and catch: releasing grip while the revolver hangs on the finger hinge throws it still turning on the same axis. Catch it with either hand and it hangs on that hand at the speed it had, until that hand presses and releases trick-shot. A bind already held at the catch does not stop it. Tossing it again repeats the throw.
- Menus, the HUD, and the prop wheel use the Duello theme: ink, paper, rust, and gold. Titles and big signs (logo, screen headers, REPLAY, the practice scoreboard, the slot machine) are set in Ultra, a heavy Western slab, and the titles and REPLAY tag carry a rust drop shadow; everything else is Bitter, with SemiBold on buttons and HUD readouts. In VR, the reload line, messages, prop wheel, loading text, and mute marker use Bitter too.
- Mannequin legs take an in-place step while a player, remote avatar, or NPC moves, and settle when they stop. In VR the local body stays hidden, so the other player is the one who sees the step.
- Mannequin fingers curl when a hand closes. In VR the grab button closes that hand (a fist if it caught nothing, otherwise the pose for the revolver, bottle, pinch prop, or belt round) and the index follows the trigger. Flat stays open until that hand is holding something. The wrist eases onto a grip marker on the object.

### Fixed

- VR: less hitching on each shot. The reload-status text in front of you is rewritten every shot, and its glyphs (digits and the outline pass) were rasterized mid-frame; they are now rendered when the label is created. The bullet slug also no longer casts a shadow, which the boot warmup never compiled. Needs a Quest frame-time check (Roadmap 1.3).
- Flat: the gun twirl works again after you win, once the replay ends or is skipped. It was refused for the rest of the round (BUG-032).
- Canyon: the desert plain below the butte no longer draws a line behind the enemy. The depth fog was capped at 1.2%, so the plain's far edge showed. It now fades into the sky colour by about 390 m, and the distant mesas fade with distance instead of drawing almost unfogged (BUG-031).
- Kill replay: an NPC shot mid-draw now raises its gun hand with its arm and keeps it on the revolver, instead of leaving the hand at its side (BUG-029).
- Holstered gun hangs barrel-down on the hip, for you and for remote avatars (BUG-028).
- Main Street: a killed duelist (an NPC, your own body, or another player's) no longer jumps into the air when it falls. Every spawn there stood 12 cm inside the ground, which threw the ragdoll out of it. ([BUG-027](docs/BUGS.md))
- Flat: when you draw or holster, your hand now moves with the revolver in the same frame, also in slow motion, where it used to trail behind. Other players see your draw the same way. ([BUG-026](docs/BUGS.md))
- A hit on the arm that is not holding the gun no longer disarms you. NPCs work the same way: only a hit on their gun arm makes them drop the gun or stops their draw. ([BUG-025](docs/BUGS.md))
- VR: you now start on your spawn mark wherever you stand in your playspace. Before, standing off the centre of your play area could start you off the Train Rooftop, outside its invisible walls. Stick walking now also stops at walls and buildings and slides along them, as in flat. Walking in your room is unchanged. ([BUG-024](docs/BUGS.md))
- Multiplayer: the `Holster your weapon` message no longer shows the enemy lineup of a single-player duel played earlier in the same session.
- Multiplayer: a rare crash when another player's avatar was removed (a rematch, a player leaving, or quitting). The voice stream was freed while the audio thread was still reading it ([BUG-021](docs/BUGS.md)).
- Quitting no longer sometimes crashes on the way out (a Godot 4.7 script-shutdown bug, worked around by a new `ScriptKeepalive` autoload). Headless autotests that passed no longer report a crash exit code ([BUG-020](docs/BUGS.md)).
- Flat: the mouse is captured as soon as a match, gauntlet rung, or Horde wave starts, so you no longer have to click before you can look around.
- NPC shots no longer pulse your controller in VR. [BUG-017]
- The Sheriff and Ghost no longer teleport sideways while strafing. [BUG-018]
- Ragdoll limbs no longer hang on the invisible walls on Train Rooftop. [BUG-019]
- No hit volume covered the upper chest and neck on the player and the remote avatar. The torso now runs up to the head.
- Mannequin knees fold the shin back on the in-place step. They were swinging the ankle toward the face, so the joint bowed backward. [BUG-016]
- The game starts again. A bad indent in the flat prop wheel script made Godot refuse the `PropRadialOverlay` class on load.
- Holding Tab opens the prop wheel. Its labels use the Duello font; the theme had no font assigned, so drawing the names crashed.
- On the replay, the dead player's mannequin retraces the draw, aim, and steps, then holds that death pose for the corpse orbit. The revolver, an off-hand prop, and a held reload round follow the clip with it. [BUG-015]
- A held object keeps the mannequin hand on it while walking or looking. The index, not the pinky, curls with the trigger.
- Mannequin fingers sit on the palm, match its thickness, and curl into the hand instead of splaying.
- The mannequin torso stays in its rest pose. Spine and chest are no longer bent toward the head; only the neck nods.
- F3 and Esc work in the mesh lab. The parked flat player no longer swallows the debug panel, and Esc opens the pause menu there.
- VR prop wheel matches the stick, and the wedge names read forwards. The ring had faced the headset backwards, so the labels were mirrored and stick-right lit the wedge on the left.
- VR **Calibrate** no longer stops the game. The headset camera in this Godot version has no tracking-data property, and reading it also blocked the next launch once a height was saved.
- In VR the arms stretch to the controllers when you reach past the mannequin’s rest length, instead of leaving the hands behind.
- In VR the hand sits on the controller, and on the revolver when it is in that hand, instead of hanging back toward the shoulder.
- In VR the hands bend at the wrist with the controller, including radial and ulnar tilt, instead of staying locked to the forearm.
- Looking down no longer shows the inside of your body. The mannequin sits just behind the eyes, and anything closer than the lens is clipped. The gun hand’s thumb points up, and the arm stays on the revolver while you look around instead of snapping to it.
- A VR session that started seated can be lined up after you stand: Settings **Reset view height** puts the headset at the saved standing height so the torso and holster meet the head. [BUG-013]
- Practice porch: a dropped revolver stays on the deck planks instead of falling through onto the lot underneath.
- VR pause no longer depends on the Meta or Steam menu buttons. The draw-hand stick click opens pause (the debug panel on the main menu). The off-hand click still opens the prop wheel. **Pause** can be rebound in Settings. Holster Side Left uses the left hand as that draw hand, including flat RMB.
- Open gate rolls the cylinder out to the side and down instead of canting it in place. The swing mirrors when the revolver changes hands.
- The off-hand bottle stands upright in the hand. `PropAttach` still aims a child's +Y forward for the cigarette; the longneck now counters that seat.

### Changed

- Train Rooftop: the four passenger cars around the duel are final untextured models, with the same collision as before. Each is a company-blue car with a flat blue tin roof and a raw-wood plank walk down its spine at the same height, bone-framed ink windows under a bone letterboard and a deep eave, open end platforms with iron railings, steps, brake wheels, and doors, dark trucks, and truss rods under the floor. The roof reaches out to the eaves with no lip, so the strafing Sheriff stays on it. A hatch, four low vents, and one sprung board sit clear of every spawn mark. The gaps between cars stay open over iron link-and-pin couplers with drooping safety chains, and a coupler now joins the last car to the engine's tender. The engine is final too: a blue, vermilion, and gold wood-burning 4-4-0 with brass domes, bell, bands, and whistle, a box headlight that glows, a diamond stack (still the highest point of the train), red driving wheels with rods, a slatted red cowcatcher, a cab with arched windows, and a tender heaped with cordwood whose sides now close up to the deck. The coupler behind the last car now meets an end beam on the tender. The engine's collision is unchanged. The two end cars are final as well, with the same collision: the baggage car behind the tender has a big blue sliding door each side with a bone Z-brace, two small high windows a side, two express hatches on the roof (one left ajar), a leaning smoke jack, and a blind end toward the engine; the business car at the very back has an open observation platform under the roof's end with a brass railing (dented at one corner), two brass marker lamps that glow (one knocked askew), wide windows between bone pilasters, and two smoke jacks. The old ladder on the last car is gone. The train is now one model per car type (`train_car_coach`, `train_coupler`, `train_engine`, `train_car_baggage`, `train_car_business`); `train_rooftop.glb` keeps only the invisible walls. The canyon walls and mesas drifting past on the horizon are final and untextured too, in the Canyon's colours: the faces the sun reaches are vermilion and the ones it never reaches bleached pink, so the rock is hot on your left and pale on your right, with one violet band along both and bone caps broken into blocks with sky between them. Each wall now steps down to a sloped apron at both ends, so neighbours read as separate formations. The canyon walls carry angled faces, a row of lit teeth on the shaded side, pillars, and fallen cap blocks; the mesas are two-tier tables with a notched top rim. The far buttes are final too: three tall stepped red posts of different heights under pale overhanging caps, each on its own sloped skirt, one cap leaning. The passenger cars' steps and truss posts no longer flicker against their hangers and rods.
- Canyon cliffs: both walls are final untextured meshes in a new palette, with the same collision as before. The west wall is vermilion and the east wall bleached pink. Each is built from horizontal strata that step in and out: thick slabs whose faces turn toward the player's end, thin set-back ledges, one violet band along both walls at about 7 to 8 m, fluted upper walls that stripe in the sun, and a bone caprock broken into teeth with sky between them. Their feet are aprons of chamfered blocks, some of them fallen bone. Pillars and the old teeth stand against the walls, and each stretch of wall carries one sign of wear: a chipped cap, a fallen block, an undercut, a crack open to the sky, or a leaning pillar block. Neither spawn mark falls in shadow at any time of day, and the Ghost's east strafe is still shaded at dawn and dusk.
- Canyon cover rocks and floor: final untextured meshes, with the same collision as before. The six cover rocks are squat violet blocks, each stepped once at mid-height under a pale bone lid that overhangs the lane side and sits slightly askew. Each has one chipped corner, and two of them are split by a crack. Their lane-facing sides stay within 15 cm of where bullets stop. The floor is one flat sheet at the duelists' feet. Its far ends break into notches away from the lane but run straight behind each duelist. Low sand banks run along some cliff feet and behind each rock, and a few low bedrock steps lie off the lane.
- Canyon rim: the butte's four sides are final untextured meshes. Each drops about 9 m in banded rock, with the violet band running round the butte 6 to 7 m below the floor, then steps out onto a ledge, drops again to a second ledge, and ends on a skirt of fallen rock on the plain 24 m down, so leaning over the rim shows the depth. The sides that take the sun are vermilion and the ones that never do are pink, the corners are cut, the top edge follows the floor's notches with no gap into the butte, and one fallen block lies on each ledge. The stones along both ends are pale fallen cap blocks, tilted, in their old places. The cliffs no longer show a hollow under their lowest slabs from the ends of the arena, or their insides from the rim's corners.
