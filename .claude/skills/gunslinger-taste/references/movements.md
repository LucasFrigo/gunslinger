# Where to look

Real makers, movements, and events to pull from. These are search leads, not a canon: look one up, look at the actual work, and bring back a specific detail. Add a line here when a search turns up a source worth returning to.

## How to search

- Search a named maker, object, or event ("Nathan Turk embroidered shirt 1940s", "L.D. Nimschke engraving pattern book"). Never "cool cowboy character design"; that returns other people's averages.
- Prefer the museum record, the archive, or the maker's own portfolio over Pinterest and listicles. Museum pages describe materials and dates, which is where the usable detail is.
- Look at the image when you can: download it to the scratchpad and read it. A caption is not the picture.
- Keep the URL. Every borrowed detail is cited in the concept card.
- Two to four lookups per session is plenty. One odd, verified detail is worth more than ten remembered ones.

## Dress

- **Rodeo tailors.** Nudie Cohn, Manuel Cuevas, Nathan Turk, Rodeo Ben. Chain-stitch embroidery, rhinestones, smile pockets with arrow tacks, piped yokes. Suits that tell the wearer's story in pictures.
- **Charro and vaquero.** Traje de charro, silver botonadura down the leg, the wide sombrero; the vaquero as the working original of the cowboy.
- **Otomi (tenango) embroidery.** Flat, bright animals and plants on plain cloth, from Hidalgo, Mexico.
- **Lucha libre masks.** A second face with its own lineage and rules about removal.
- **Votive imagery.** Sacred hearts, milagros, ex-voto paintings, Posada's calavera prints.
- **Drag and stage western.** Glamour used as threat; horror hosts.
- **Workwear.** Dusters, chaps, wild rags, sugarloaf and Boss of the Plains hats, spur and bit makers (Garcia, Crockett).

## Print and picture

- **Survey photography.** Timothy O'Sullivan, William Henry Jackson, Carleton Watkins. Tintypes and cabinet cards of ordinary frontier people.
- **Letterpress and bills.** Hatch Show Print, wood type, wanted posters, medicine-show handbills, circus and Wild West show posters.
- **Pulp.** Dime-novel covers; 1950s western comics with Ben-Day dots.
- **Film posters.** Italian spaghetti-western sheets; the Polish poster school's westerns.
- **Painters.** Frederic Remington's nocturnes, Maynard Dixon's skies, N.C. Wyeth.
- **Comics and illustration.** Hirohiko Araki, Yoshitaka Amano, Jean Giraud's *Blueberry*, Sergio Toppi, Hugo Pratt.

## Film and games (for tone, not plot)

- Leone and Corbucci: *Django* (1966), *The Great Silence* (a snow western).
- Acid and anti-westerns: *El Topo*, *Dead Man*, *High Plains Drifter*.
- Weird West: *Vampire Hunter D*, *Trigun*, *Fallout: New Vegas* (Joshua Graham, Benny), *Hard West*.

## Guns as objects

- Presentation pieces: Tiffany & Co. grips, engravers L.D. Nimschke and Gustave Young.
- Odd mechanisms: LeMat (nine shots plus a shotgun barrel), pepperboxes, the Volcanic pistol, the Apache knuckleduster revolver, cap-and-ball conversions.

## History and folklore (for lore)

- Duelling itself: the Irish Code Duello of 1777 and its seconds; Hickok and Tutt in Springfield, 1865, the usual model for the quick-draw duel.
- People the films left out: Bass Reeves, Nat Love, Stagecoach Mary Fields, Chinese railroad crews, Buffalo Soldiers, vaqueros.
- Institutions: Pinkertons, railroad companies, cattle associations, travelling medicine shows, Buffalo Bill's Wild West.
- Feuds: the Lincoln County War, the El Paso Salt War.
- Belief: Santa Muerte, La Llorona, the Penitente brotherhoods, Day of the Dead.
